# Lesson 2.2 - Relative Frequency Distribution Table

> **Video:** [How To Make a Relative Frequency Distribution Table](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gq3FPpm2yvA) by The Organic Chemistry Tutor
> **Chapter:** [2 - Graphs and Tables](https://www.video-tutor.net/graphs-and-tables.html)

## Idea

**Relative frequency** is the **fraction** (or percentage) of the data taken up by each value:

$$\text{relative frequency} = \frac{f}{\sum f} = \frac{f}{n}$$

## Steps

1. Make a frequency table: **V** (value) and **f** (frequency).
2. Add the frequency column to get $n$.
3. Add a third column **RF** = $f / n$.
4. **Check:** the RF column must add up to **1** (or 100%).

---

## Example

Data (20 values): 2, 5, 3, 4, 7, 2, 5, 8, 3, 2, 2, 3, 2, 5, 5, 4, 8, 5, 2, 8

| V | f | RF = f / 20 |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 6 | 0.30 |
| 3 | 3 | 0.15 |
| 4 | 2 | 0.10 |
| 5 | 5 | 0.25 |
| 7 | 1 | 0.05 |
| 8 | 3 | 0.15 |
| **Total** | **20** | **1.00** |

There is no 6 in the data, so 6 is left out of the table.

**Reading the table:**

- The relative frequency of the value **4** is **0.10**, so 10% of the data are 4s.
- The relative frequency of the value **8** is **0.15**, so 15% of the data are 8s.

**Check:** $0.30 + 0.15 + 0.10 + 0.25 + 0.05 + 0.15 = 1.00$ ✓

---

## Key takeaways

- RF = frequency ÷ total frequency.
- RF values are between 0 and 1 and always **sum to 1**. If they don't, recount.
- Multiply by 100 to read RF as a percentage.
- The next step is the **cumulative relative frequency** (Lesson 2.3).
